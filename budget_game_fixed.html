Can You Build Sri Lanka’s 2027 Budget?

You have ten weeks to prepare Sri Lanka’s 2027 Budget. You are the Finance Minister, and the decisions are yours to make.

You’re the Finance Minister, and you have 10 weeks to build Sri Lanka’s 2027 Budget! You have around Rs. 5 trillion to spend to spend excluding interest payments. You must decide how the Government raises money and where it spends it. Can you support the economy, fund essential public services and keep the country’s debt under control?

But here’s the catch: you can’t fund everything at once. Spend more on one priority, and you may have less for another. Your choices will affect government revenue, spending, borrowing needs and the wider economy.

ⓘ Source & assumptionsWhere the figures come from. Every starting figure in this game comes from the 2027 Appropriation Bill baseline, IMF projections for 2027, or game assumptions, and each game assumption is clearly labelled.

Economic context. Sri Lanka’s economy is growing, but many families continue to face high living costs. Inflation reached 8% in September 2026, with food inflation at 8.7%. Poverty and inequality remain important challenges, and economic growth has not benefited everyone equally.

Economic assumptions for 2027. Real GDP growth 3.2% and inflation 5.3% (IMF projections; inflation was about 8% in Aug–Sep 2026). The rupee weakens 4% over the year and the 1-year Treasury bill rate is about 9.25%.

Natural growth. With no policy change, revenue grows with nominal GDP (real growth plus inflation, about 8.7%). Spending would need to grow as fast to keep its share of GDP. The 2027 Appropriation Bill sets spending before interest at Rs. 4,993 bn, only about 1% above this year, so every sector's share of GDP falls unless you raise it. Every slider starts at the Bill's figure (0%) and moves spending by that percentage.

Interest. About 6% of GDP and mostly outside your control. The Treasury bill rate drives rupee borrowing costs; the exchange rate drives foreign interest and border taxes. More interest means less cash for other priorities.

Limits. Spending before interest must not exceed 13% of the GDP projected for 2027 (Public Financial Management Act, No. 44 of 2024), and the Bill is already close to it. A large capital allocation uses up room that could fund spending more certain to be delivered, and capital projects have often been under-spent. How the deficit is financed is not part of this game.

Targets. Revenue 15.1% of GDP, primary surplus 2.3%, public investment 4.0%, deficit no more than 3.7%, and the 13% spending ceiling. These are IMF projections (Country Report 26/111), because the Government's own 2027 targets come in the Budget Speech on 12 November 2026. Baselines are from the 2027 Appropriation Bill (7 October 2026). Growth, inflation, interest and tax effects of your choices are simplified game assumptions, not forecasts.

Your challenge

There is no perfect Budget. Spend too much, and the deficit may rise. Cut too much, and essential services and investment may suffer. Raise taxes, and you may collect more revenue, but households and businesses could face greater costs.

Make your choices, watch the numbers change, and see whether you can build a Budget that meets people’s needs today while protecting Sri Lanka’s economic future.

Taxes

Before deciding how to spend the money, decide how the Government will raise it. With no change in policy, revenue grows with the economy to about Rs. 5,900 bn in 2027 (15.2% of GDP in 2026, grown by about 8.7%). The IMF projects revenue of 15.1% of GDP for 2027, and the Government is also improving tax collection through the Revenue Administration Management Information System. What, if anything, will you add to that?
